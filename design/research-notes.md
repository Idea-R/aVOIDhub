# aVOID visual reference notes

Reviewed: 2026-08-17

The references are inputs, not templates. aVOID has eight known games, so it should not imitate the density or merchandising machinery of a storefront with thousands of titles.

## Playdate Catalog

- Useful: tactile category cards, unapologetic typography, playful controls, and a directory that feels like the product.
- Adapt: make filters and game states collectible and physical without becoming childish.
- Avoid: too many novelty type treatments competing with the game art.

## Apple Arcade

- Useful: a single game can temporarily own the canvas; motion and artwork communicate play before copy does.
- Adapt: let one featured aVOID game set the hero mood, with a fast escape into the full catalog.
- Avoid: subscription-first messaging and heavy video before the catalog earns it.

## Epic Games Store

- Useful: quiet navigation chrome, large art surfaces, and obvious browsing/search hierarchy.
- Adapt: give game artwork room and keep account controls secondary.
- Avoid: anonymous dark-store sameness and excessive empty skeleton states.

## Xbox Game Pass

- Useful: direct calls to action and unmistakable section hierarchy.
- Adapt: every primary action should say exactly what happens next.
- Avoid: corporate masthead weight, repeated subscription pressure, and generic centered marketing slabs.

## IndieList

- Useful: editorial hierarchy, visible evidence, restrained color, and a discovery surface that explains why an item appears.
- Adapt: show whether a game is hosted, external, or coming soon; never hide provenance.
- Avoid: making the small aVOID catalog feel like a database administration tool.

## Gamoola

- Useful: scale, silhouette, motion confidence, and a hero with one memorable gesture.
- Adapt: reserve one unusual canvas/WebGL moment for the void-field hero.
- Avoid: letting the spectacle obscure navigation, game launches, or mobile performance.

## Six directions to explore

1. **Editorial Arcade** — evolve the coded baseline: warm paper, teal, black, offset cards, oversized type, and highly legible catalog states.
2. **Cabinet Wall** — a dark physical arcade with illuminated cabinet tiles, angled labels, and tactile hover depth.
3. **Signal Index** — an evidence-forward directory and leaderboard with dark olive, burnt orange, clean data typography, and honest game-state badges.
4. **Playable Poster** — one featured game owns the hero like a kinetic film poster, with the remaining catalog stacked underneath.
5. **Soft Hardware** — light, friendly, deliberately chunky controls and category tiles; playful without looking like a children’s product.
6. **Void Terminal** — black-and-ivory graphic system with cyan/orange signals, radar lines, modular panels, and one restrained generative background.

## Shared constraints

- Show the aVOID games directory as the primary product.
- Include a leaderboard/profile signal without implying they are already live.
- Keep TankaVOID visibly blocked as coming soon.
- Mark externally hosted Ideas Realized games clearly.
- Design desktop and mobile as peers.
- No fake player counts, fake testimonials, token systems, or pay-to-win cues.
